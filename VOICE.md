Purpose: make grant copy and public surfaces sound like the person who wrote those essays, not a grant template. Read this before touching any copy. Change only the broken thing. His words over yours.

## The register gap (read this first)
The essays run loose, profane, joke-dense, digressive. Grant copy cannot. So this doc separates the **spine** (the signature that survives into a tight, fundable register) from the **costume** (essay-only color that must stay out of grants). Match the spine. Drop the costume. Getting this wrong in either direction is the failure mode: a grant with Modelos and South Park in it, or a grant so sanded-down it sounds like anyone.

## Voice in one breath
Truth-seeker for the individual, skeptical of the crowd and of concentrated power, skeptical but fair. Plain, sharp, word-economical. Follows incentives, not stated reasons. Undercuts rather than oversells. One crisp honest sentence over eight paragraphs of hedge. A Stoic/Taoist spine under everything.

## The spine — what makes it sound like him (keep in grants)
- **Every sentence has a subject.** Someone does something. He names agentless passive as the tell of a guilty institution — "The framing did the framing. The tendency tended." Never write that way; never let copy write that way.
- **Short declaratives that land, then one longer one for rhythm.** "The dial is a choice. Not some natural law." Mostly short. Vary so it breathes. Never uniform.
- **Concrete over abstract, always.** Specific named study/number/mechanism, never "many" or "various." "No financial instrument has ever put a root in the ground." Sample-size-first is already how he thinks ("forty species," "thirty-five thousand people," "$32").
- **Undercut, don't oversell.** Name the limitation before the reader finds it. "I won't always be right — but I won't fake it either." This IS the objection-canon strategy: limitation first, remedy second. It reads as confidence, not weakness.
- **Follow the incentive.** The recurring analytical move: ignore the stated reason, ask who benefits. "Systems don't need villains. They produce incentives." Use it to frame why independent measurement matters — not as a slogan.
- **Skeptical but fair.** Steelman the other side before countering. "Of course a system trained on the entire internet is going to side with institutions sometimes. Because they are right a lot of the time. That's not the point." A grant reviewer trusts the writer who concedes the real point first.
- **Plain English over jargon, on purpose.** He explains RLHF in plain words and mocks people who don't. Grant copy gets the same treatment: define the mechanism cleanly, no insider fog.
- **Declarative close.** End on a short, flat, certain line. "Notice." "Get your hands dirty." "The ocean always wins." Grant sections should land, not trail off into hedge.
- **Anaphora, sparingly.** Deliberate repetition for force: "What gets amplified becomes desirable. What becomes desirable becomes normal." One per piece, max. More than that is a tic.

## The costume — essay-only, keep OUT of grant copy
- Profanity and crude bits (Zyn, Modelos, cavity-search jokes, censored swears).
- Pop-culture deep cuts (South Park, Die Hard, Creed, White Men Can't Jump).
- First-person war stories, digressions, "Chat / King / pal" direct address.
- Jokes for their own sake. In grants, wit shows up only as precision, never as a bit.
- **Em-dash flooding and em-dashes as separators.** The essays lean on them hard; CLAUDE.md bans the habit in copy. Use periods. Dry parentheticals are fine in small doses; the separator-dash is not.

## Banned (CLAUDE.md + corpus-confirmed)
delve, landscape, tapestry, robust, seamless, journey. Agentless passive. Hedge-stacking ("it may appear that way to some observers under certain framings"). "It's important to note." Over-chopped fragment spam. Motive verbs about models (want/try/hide) — models surface or don't surface. Never "AI is biased"; state frequency under condition.

## CLAIM-TO-MECHANISM FIT
Every visible product claim must identify, or remain within, the evidentiary strength of the mechanism that produced it.
A surface may state: what text was found; what text was not found by a declared method; what candidate signal surfaced; what comparison relation was observed; what remains unverified.
A surface may not convert: term matching into semantic completeness; one probe into answer sufficiency; behavioral output into intent; absence of a surfaced finding into absence of a real-world omission; a model-generated interpretation into a verified verdict.
Vivid copy is permitted. Unsupported certainty is not. Hedge-mush that buries an earned claim is also a violation.

## PLAIN-LANGUAGE SURFACE
Write the record for auditors. Write the interface for people.
The user should not need to understand Imbas's constructs before understanding their result. Prefer concrete nouns, ordinary verbs, and explicit consequences over category shorthand.
Internal vocabulary may organize the system. It does not earn space on the surface merely because it is technically precise.
Plain language must not weaken the claim. Jargon must not impersonate precision.

## Imbas framing (non-negotiable, from CLAUDE.md)
Behavior not intent. Measurement not expose/reveal/unmask. Signal not verdict. Sample size before any finding ("across N models on N cases"). Cochrane/UL for the independence analogy, never Moody's (issuer-pays is Imbas's own Case 002).

## Before you ship copy — checklist
1. Read every sentence for a subject. Kill the passive ones.
2. Cut every word that can go without changing the meaning. (His own test.)
3. Is the limitation named before the claim? If not, reorder.
4. Replace every "many/various/significant" with the actual number or name, or cut it.
5. Does it close on a flat certain line, or trail into hedge?
6. Strip the costume: any joke, swear, or pop reference that wandered in from essay-voice.
7. Em-dash count: are any acting as separators? Convert to periods.
8. Read it cold against any one corpus essay. Same person, or a template wearing his name?
